Tubedash

A private dashboard of new videos from the YouTube channels I follow, with a short summary of each and a button to save a video to my YouTube playlist.

What it does

  • New videos in one place. Tubedash checks the public feeds of the channels I follow every 15 minutes and lists new uploads, with each creator's description.
  • Short summaries on request. When I click Summarize, Google's Gemini API watches the public video and writes a 3 to 5 line gist, so I can decide what's worth watching.
  • Save to YouTube. A Save button adds a video to a playlist named “TubeDash” on my YouTube channel, so it shows up in the YouTube app and on my TV. Clicking it again removes the video.
  • Watched and filters. Mark videos watched, and filter by channel, date, Shorts or saved videos.

Who it's for

Tubedash is a personal tool. I run it for myself on a private server that only my own devices can reach. It isn't offered as a service, has no public sign-up and isn't available to anyone else.

How it uses Google data

Saving to YouTube is the only part that uses my Google account. When I connect YouTube, Google asks me to allow Tubedash to manage my YouTube account, which is the narrowest permission Google offers for changing a playlist. With it, Tubedash only:

  • reads my channel's name, to show which account is connected;
  • reads my playlists, to find the one named “TubeDash”;
  • lists, adds and removes videos in that one playlist.

It never uploads, deletes or edits videos, never touches other playlists, comments, subscriptions or watch history, and never shares or sells any data. The details are in the privacy policy.

Tubedash uses YouTube API Services. By using it you agree to the YouTube Terms of Service; see also the Google Privacy Policy.
